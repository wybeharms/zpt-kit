# Intake questions

The interview the agent runs in the Orient step (Step 1 of `guide.md`), woven through the ground work. The agent asks proactively, in small batches the person answers in one message. The person should not have to know what context to volunteer; drawing it out is the agent's job, and it is the single biggest reason the finished setup actually fits.

How the agent uses this file:

- **Small batches.** Up to five questions at once, answered in one message. Follow up on the interesting answers.
- **Pick the right phrasing.** Step 0 already answered personal vs professional. Professional: use the work phrasings. Personal: use the personal ones. A student's "work" is school: classes, applications, sports, activities.
- **Skip what is already answered.** Step 0 covered the tool, the platform, experience level, and the goal; the Orient sweep found the locations. When improving, the setup's own files answer a lot too; read before asking. Do not re-ask anything.
- **Follow threads.** A good answer opens a better follow-up than the next scripted question.
- **The person may always shrug.** "You decide" is a valid answer: pick a default, state it in one line, move on.
- **Stop when there is enough** to draw a sensible structure (and, when improving, to judge the current setup against the standard). Conversation, not a form.
- **Capture the answers.** The agent records them in `session-notes.md` as they come, and later in the setup's instructions and context files.

## A. The person  *(essential)*

- Work: name, role, and who they work with day to day (team, clients, partners).
- Personal: name, who is in the picture (family, household, the people they organize things for).
- Student: name, year, school, and what fills the week besides class.

> Sets who the agent is helping, and the name to use instead of pronouns.

## B. The time  *(essential)*

- Work: what takes the most time in a normal week? What repeats? What does a typical output look like (document, analysis, email, spreadsheet)? What is high-stakes and must be done carefully?
- Personal: what takes up the week (planning, admin, paperwork, logistics)? What gets redone over and over? What deadlines cause stress?
- Student: which assignments or applications repeat? What gets rewritten from scratch every time?

## C. Goals and pain  *(essential)*

Step 0's goal answer seeds this section; deepen it here instead of re-asking it.

- If the agent could reliably do one thing well, what would save the most time or stress?
- What has the person tried with AI before that did not work?
- When improving: what was this setup supposed to do when it was built, and is it doing it? What have they tried with it that did not work, or that they gave up on?
- What would a good result look like a month from now?

## D. What already exists, beyond what the sweep finds  *(essential when improving)*

The sweep and the ground work read the files; these questions catch what files cannot say.

- Which parts of the setup do they actually use, and which are abandoned?
- Are there skills or workflows they run regularly? Any they built and never used again?
- Are tools connected (connectors, MCPs) such as email, a drive, or a CRM? Used, or just wired up?
- Are there conventions they follow on purpose (naming, structure, where things go), even unusual ones? (Deliberate conventions stay; see `what-good-looks-like.md`, "Intentional exceptions.")

## E. Rules and boundaries  *(important)*

- Anything the agent should never do?
- Anything that must **never live in these folders**? (Passwords never. Also worth naming: medical, financial, and other people's private information; see `key-concepts.md`, "What never goes in the folder.")
- Confidentiality: names, clients, or data that must stay inside the folder?
- Preferred tone, output formats, or naming conventions?

## F. Materials  *(helpful)*

- A few real examples of the work from section B (documents, letters, plans), or of good output the setup should be producing. The agent uses them as reference only and does not change them.

---

The goal, familiarity with agents, and personal vs professional are gauged in Step 0 of `guide.md`, and the setup locations come from the Orient sweep; none of that is asked here. When the answers are in: straight to Propose, show the structure unprompted.
